Stoicism is an often misunderstood concept in the modern day landscape of the internet. Often than not, we view the philosophy stance as somewhat selfish or being apathetic. And that is when I decided to pick up a book called "Being Better: Stoicism for a World Worth Living In" By Kai Whiting and Leonidas Konstantakos to figure out what is the true meaning of being a stoic:

**Four Stoic virtues:**

1. *Courage*
2. *Justice*
3. *Self-control*
4. *Wisdom*

To be **Courageous** we shall live bravely and seek uncomfortable interactions and make hard decisions in the name of a greater good.

To be **Just** is to be fair; to take time to research and deeply consider different aspect of a story such as people's characters, standing in society or workplace and weed out any bias in order to come up with a solution to disagreements.

To be **Self-controlled** is to be aware and intentionally regulate eating, sex, entertainment and work to the extend not to harm oneself or the environment of which we live in.

To be **Wise** is a very difficult task, one might need to learn but more importantly to experiment with life to gather the amount of wisdom so that they can judge right from wrong morally. 

By considering these four key pillars of what makes a stoic, it is made clear that what I've heard about stoicism through short-form content and social media is a narrow view on a much more complicated topic than before.
