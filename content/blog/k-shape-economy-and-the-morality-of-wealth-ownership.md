During one of my recent breakfast talks with my mum, she stressed how the ownership of wealth can be very toxic, both for yourself as an individual and for the people around you. Hence, the moral thing for anyone who has accumulated wealth is to give a large portion of it away to people who need it more.

It is particularly interesting to me, as her generation grew up in a significantly different economy from mine. Vietnam has only been reunified for a little more than 50 years, and our modern market economy is still relatively young. However, the key difference between the economy of the past and what we are going through now can perhaps be explained by the term "K-shaped economy."

To put it simply, the obsession of many countries around the world with increasing GDP in order to race towards becoming the next superpower has exposed a major flaw in the system: GDP does not take into consideration the cost of living, quality of life, or the happiness of its people.

As a result, wealth distribution and stagnant wages have been pushed to the sidelines in favor of economic growth. People who find themselves owning assets suddenly find themselves in a much better financial position. Conversely, people whose jobs are not as well paid find their day-to-day lives becoming increasingly expensive and unaffordable due to the rising cost of living and inflation. This effectively locks them into perpetual financial hardship, as much of the wealth creation seems to be concentrated in the asset market (i.e., stocks, investment funds, real estate, etc.).

Coming back to my mum's advice, when I was growing up, I was taught to be nice to other people and help those who are in need. And for the longest time, that seemed like a fairly straightforward thing to do. But the conundrum now is: What if, by helping other people, I end up locking myself out of owning assets and, as a result, prevent myself from achieving financial freedom?

Has the world become a place where everyone is for themselves? Where deciding to be the better person only results in you being the one who bears the consequences?

As a follower of Stoic philosophy and a strong believer in game theory, I think there is still a chance for good people to thrive.

As the famous "Prisoner's Dilemma" goes, while acting selfishly might seem like the more rational choice for an individual, a better collective outcome can be achieved when both parties choose to cooperate. Of course, this only really works when people are willing to reciprocate. Being a good person doesn't mean you have to let everyone take advantage of you.

When the world's economic system seems to be increasingly advantageous to those who act selfishly, it becomes even more important for good people to rise up and stick together.

Because deep within the system towards which our world is moving, there seems to be an increasing reliance on people becoming more distant from one another. Neighbors no longer talk to each other, friends are unable to meet up as often, and human connections are becoming increasingly artificial and synthetic.

And perhaps that is what makes this whole situation even more concerning to me. We are being pushed towards an economy where individual financial security matters more than ever, while slowly losing the very relationships that might help us get through difficult times.

Which is why, in order to beat the system, we as individuals must look towards helping others, making sure that other people's hardships and misfortunes are shared and understood. This doesn't necessarily mean giving away everything we have or sacrificing our own financial security in the process. But as long as we are willing to help carry someone else's misfortune, perhaps our own future misfortunes will no longer be ours to bear alone.

After all, what good is financial freedom if, by the time we achieve it, we find ourselves having no one left to share it with?

Finally, is all of this to say that things will eventually pass? I don't really know. And perhaps waiting for things to get better isn't the right approach in the first place.

In game theory, the most vulnerable player in a population of bad actors is a lonely good one. In contrast, one of the strongest collectives is a community of good actors who are willing to cooperate and look out for one another.

So instead of closing ourselves off and waiting for the storm to pass, potentially getting blown away in the process, why not find other people who are willing to weather the storm with us and collectively make each other's lives just that much better?
