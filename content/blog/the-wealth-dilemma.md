Every morning, Me and my mom usually spend some time to sit together and have breakfast together, it usually goes like this: I wake up, mom started to plate the food, i came downstairs, then we choose what to play on the television then we eat then talk about what is on my mind that day. In today's repast, a question arises: "How can one continue to thrive when faced with convenience and comfort?"  
If you think about it, the better fed and wealth an individual is, the less "hunger" that individual has to get out of their "comfort zone". What happens when someone who "has everything" and become the person who they thrived to be many years ago? Leaving success with a true bitter sweetness of the question "What next?". Some may choose to stop and enjoy life, but if you spend all your time enjoying life then what is left to actually feel the enjoyment after a while?

WORK LIFE BALANCE

Work life balance has increasingly became a hotly debated topic for many young people. And definitely FOMO is the key argument against the fact that if you stop then someone will catch up and surpass you. It is in a sense true but depending on what are your priorities it might be a good thing. A society full of competition can give people the hunger to do better and to keep improving themselves. However, it is imperative that the rivalry must come from a place of kindness and caring not hatred and spite. 

The question of WEALTH  
A previous breakfast discussion with my mom we talked about how different wage group viewed money and goods differently. The question came up when i was buying me and my mom a freshly baked baguette at a local groceries store and there was 2 choices, one was available for less than a dollar, made with cheaper ingredients, has the same calories facts but taste a bit worse than the other choice that cost slightly more than a dollar. The most intriguing fact about it that thanks to my unique background as i was born into poverty and my parents pulled us out into the middle class, i noticed that my point of view on such pricing would changed compared to the past. 

- For the lower income household: the cheaper bread is a no-brainer since it cost less, weigh the same and can feed the same amount of people.
- For the middle income household: Sure the cheaper bread is great value but it is only a few dongs extra for an authentic stick of sour dough baguette.
- For the high income household: I get to eat breakfast only once a day so I will get the best choice money can buy as to make life more enjoyable. 

Of course the high income house hold point of view is purely speculation from me. 



All-in-all, a person can be rich while poor and can be poor while rich. A key take away from my mother that she always stress in these talks is to always donate or to give your extras to the people who need it as to keep the hunger and motivation for yourself to improve and become better men/women.
