The Internet (also known as "the Web") is a sophisticated, interconnected network of computers all over the world. Humanity as a whole has been propelled to new heights. Innovations, ideas, and culture were once restricted by geography but now flow freely across the cyber world. So HOW did we make it so easily accessible and quick?

The first component that contributes to the speedy nature of information is the ingenious use of protocols, namely TCP and UDP. When we connect to a website, for example, the UDP protocol is one of the ways we interact with modern data streams. Thanks to how fast connectivity has become over the last decade, data access has become available virtually in real time! Here's how the protocols work: the TCP protocol guides our computer to reach out to the host server until a "handshake" is made, and the two machines establish a constant, reliable connection when sending data. Conversely, the UDP protocol allows both machines to send information to each other constantly without taking the time to confirm whether the other party has received it or not. This results in a fast but unreliable connection, right? Actually, thanks to how fast our connections have become, the effects are often negligible, and any missing data can be compensated for by the client machine to create a near-perfect illusion of real-time communication.

Of course, we cannot just credit smart protocols alone for how fast our data flows. Optimization is another key aspect for the system to function efficiently. The Internet consists of a network of ISPs (Internet Service Providers), with individual machines connecting to their ISP of choice. That opens up the ability to optimize routing on a micro scale, which ultimately contributes to how fast the whole system can be!

Last but not least, the physical layer – how do we physically connect the world together? The invention of fiber-optic cables was a turning point and one of the keys to unlocking the potential of near-instant connections. Gone are the days when we were held back by how conductive our cables were. Now, thanks to fiber-optic technology, our data travels at nearly the speed of light. However, physically extending fiber-optic cables still remains a difficult task due to geopolitics and geography. Places with access enjoy fast connections, while those without it still suffer from subpar speeds.
